Battles Scheduler helps you answer a familiar team-management question: who should play each match when everyone has different availability?

Add your roster and match times, mark who can attend, and let Battles Scheduler build lineups that spread opportunities fairly. You can adjust the result, copy name lists, or download share-ready images.

<Note>
	Battles Scheduler is free, works without an account, and keeps every schedule in your browser.
</Note>

![The Battles Scheduler Board before simulation, showing a player pool, ten match-time containers, and lineup settings](../images/scheduler/scheduler-board.png)

## Quick start

<Steps>
	<Step title="Open Battles Scheduler">
		Go to [teambattles.gg/scheduler](https://teambattles.gg/scheduler). Use a screen at least
		1024 pixels wide to edit; smaller screens show a read-only lineup view.
	</Step>
	<Step title="Choose how to begin">
		Start blank, copy a schedule already on this device, import a `.tbsched` file, or load
		the editable example schedule.
	</Step>
	<Step title="Add availability">
		Create your match times, add players, and mark who can play each time slot. You can work
		from the visual **Board** or the spreadsheet-like **Grid**.
	</Step>
	<Step title="Create the lineups">
		Choose your seat count and fairness preference, then select **Simulate**. Review the
		lineups, notes, blockers, and fairness summary.
	</Step>
	<Step title="Share or save the result">
		Copy the lineup names, download lineup-card images, or export a `.tbsched` backup.
	</Step>
</Steps>

## What Battles Scheduler considers

The simulation balances the information you provide:

- Who can, might, must, or cannot play each time slot.
- How many starters each time slot needs.
- Whether time slots overlap or are too close together.
- Whether players should be kept together or apart.
- Who can host or act as IGL.
- Whether you want an even number of starts or an even share of each player's opportunities.

You stay in control. You can move players manually, run another variation, reset the computed lineups, or undo the entire simulation.
